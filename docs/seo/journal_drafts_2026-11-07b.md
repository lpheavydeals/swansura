**Article text:**

A good **moisturiser** is the step that holds the rest of your routine together. It softens skin, helps it feel comfortable all day and gives make-up or sunscreen a smooth base. But the right texture depends on your skin: a rich cream that rescues dry skin can feel heavy on oily skin, and a light lotion may not be enough in winter. Here is how to choose a moisturiser for your skin type.

## Cream, lotion or gel: what is the difference?

Most moisturisers mix water with oils and butters. The more oil and butter, the richer the texture:

- **Rich creams** hold more butters and oils, such as shea butter and almond oil. They suit dry and very dry skin, and cold weather.
- **Light creams and lotions** have more water and lighter oils. They suit normal, oily and combination skin.
- **Gels and serums** are mostly water with moisture-loving ingredients such as glycerine and hyaluronic acid. They work on their own for oily skin, or under a cream.

## How to apply moisturiser

- **Cleanse** with a face wash that suits your skin: see [face wash for your skin type](/blogs/journal/face-wash-for-your-skin-type).
- **Apply serum,** if you use one, to slightly damp skin.
- **Warm a pea-sized amount** of moisturiser between your fingertips.
- **Press and smooth** it over your face and neck with upward strokes, avoiding the eyes.
- **In the morning, finish with sunscreen** unless your moisturiser has SPF.

## What makes a moisturiser "natural"?

There is no legal definition of "natural" in the UK, so read the ingredient list rather than the label. ## Good to know

- **Patch test** a new moisturiser on your jaw or inner arm for 24 hours.
- **Your skin changes with the seasons:** many people switch to a richer cream in winter and a lighter one in summer.
- **Dryness, redness or itching that lasts** is worth showing to a pharmacist or your GP.

A **face mask** is the easiest way to give your skin ten minutes of extra care. Once or twice a week, it can leave skin feeling fresher, softer or more comfortable, depending on the type you choose. Here are the main kinds of face mask, which suits your skin, and how to use one.

## The main types of face mask

- **Clay masks** use clays such as kaolin or French pink clay. They dry down on the skin and feel deep-cleansing, which makes them popular with oily and combination skin.
- **Charcoal masks** add activated charcoal, often with tea tree, for a fresh, clarifying feel.
- **Powder face packs** are dry blends of plant powders that you mix with water, rose water or aloe gel just before use.
- **Cream and gel masks** are hydrating and stay soft on the skin, which suits dry and tired-looking skin.

## Which face mask suits your skin?

- **Oily or combination skin:** clay or charcoal masks, on the T-zone or the whole face.
- **Dry skin:** a hydrating cream mask, or a creamy clay mask such as the Milk Mud Mask, rinsed off before it dries completely.
- **Sensitive skin:** a short ingredient list without added fragrance, and a patch test first.
- **Not sure?** Try multi-masking: clay on the oily areas, a hydrating mask on the dry ones.

## How to use a face mask

- **Cleanse first** with a face wash that suits your skin: see [face wash for your skin type](/blogs/journal/face-wash-for-your-skin-type).
- **Apply an even layer** with clean fingertips or a brush, avoiding the eyes and lips.
- **Leave it on** for the time on the pack, usually 10 to 15 minutes. Rinse clay masks before they crack and pull, so skin does not feel tight.
- **Rinse with lukewarm water** and a soft cloth, then pat dry.
- **Follow with toner and moisturiser** straight away. Aloe vera gel is a light option: read [how to use aloe vera gel](/blogs/journal/how-to-use-aloe-vera-gel).

## How often should you use a face mask?

For most people, clay and charcoal masks **once or twice a week** are plenty; dry or sensitive skin may prefer once a week. Hydrating masks can be used more often. Always follow the directions on the pack.

## Good to know

- **Patch test** any new mask on your jaw or inner arm first.
- **Turmeric can leave a faint yellow tint** on skin and towels; rinse well and use an old towel.
- **Skip masks** on broken, sunburnt or irritated skin. Breakouts or redness that last are worth showing to a pharmacist or your GP.

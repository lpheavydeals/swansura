Paco Rabanne's fragrance names can be confusing: 1 Million, Invictus and Phantom for men, Fame, Lady Million and Olympéa for women, each in several versions. Here is every **Paco Rabanne perfume** line we stock, with its notes, the versions on offer and who each one suits.

## Paco Rabanne for him

It is sweet, spiced and confident, a natural choice for nights out and colder weather. The 1 Million Elixir, which you will find in the [men's miniature set](/products/paco-rabanne-mens-miniature-set-phantom-invictus-1-million-elixir-1-million-4-x-5ml) (£50.99, 4 x 5ml), takes a different route: apple and davana, then Damask rose, osmanthus and cedar, over vanilla absolute, tonka bean and patchouli.

### Invictus: fresh and marine

[Paco Rabanne Invictus Eau de Toilette](/products/paco-rabanne-invictus-eau-de-toilette-200ml) (£78.99, 200ml) is the fresh one: sea notes, grapefruit and mandarin orange, then bay leaf and jasmine, over ambergris, guaiac wood, oakmoss and patchouli. It suits everyday wear, the office and warmer days. [Invictus Victory Eau de Parfum Extrême](/products/paco-rabanne-invictus-victory-eau-de-parfum-extreme-100ml) (£65.99, 100ml) is the warmer sister: pink pepper and lemon, then olibanum and lavender, over vanilla, tonka bean and amber. ### Phantom: aromatic lavender

Phantom is built around lavender. The eau de toilette, found in both men's miniature sets, opens with lavender, lemon zest and Amalfi lemon, adds apple and a smoky, earthy heart, and dries down to vanilla, lavender and vetiver. ### XS Black: woody and herbal

[Paco Rabanne XS Black Eau de Toilette](/products/paco-rabanne-xs-black-eau-de-toilette-100ml) (£44.99, 100ml) mixes mint, bergamot and lemon with juniper berries, coriander and geranium, over oakmoss, sandalwood and rosemary. It is the most herbal and woody of the men's range.

# Foot cream: how to look after dry, rough feet at home

/blogs/journal/foot-cream-guide · tag Skin Studio · read 3 min

**SEO title:** Foot Cream for Dry Feet: How to Choose and Use It | Swansura  
**Meta description:** How to choose a foot cream or foot butter for dry, rough feet, plus a simple at-home pedicure with a foot soak and scrub.


Feet have thicker skin than the rest of the body and few oil glands, so they are often the first place to feel dry, especially in winter, when boots, tights and central heating all take their toll. A good **foot cream**, used regularly, keeps heels and soles feeling soft and comfortable. Here is how to choose one, and how to fit it into a simple at-home pedicure.

## Why feet get so dry

The skin on your heels and soles takes your weight all day. It is naturally thick, and when it dries out it can feel rough and tight. Hot baths, long showers, walking barefoot on hard floors and closed shoes in heated rooms all make it drier. Moisturising little and often works better than an occasional rescue.

## Foot cream or foot butter?

- **A foot cream** contains water as well as oils and butters, so it sinks in quickly. It suits daily use, morning or night.

- **A foot butter** or balm has no water: it is a solid blend of oils, butters and wax that sits on the skin for longer. It suits very dry heels and works well at night under cotton socks.

## Everyday habits for comfortable feet

- **Moisturise daily,** ideally after a bath or shower, while skin is still slightly damp.

- **Keep showers warm, not hot,** and do not leave feet soaking for long.

- **Wear socks** around the house in winter rather than walking barefoot on cold, hard floors.

- **Do not use foot cream between the toes;** keep that skin dry.

## FAQs

**What is the difference between a foot cream and a foot butter?**  
A foot cream contains water as well as oils and butters, so it sinks in quickly and suits daily use. A foot butter has no water: it is a solid blend of oils, butters and wax that stays on the skin for longer, which suits very dry heels and night-time use under cotton socks.

**How often should I use foot cream?**  
Once or twice a day, ideally after a bath or shower and before bed. Moisturising little and often keeps feet more comfortable than an occasional rich treatment.

**How often should I scrub my feet?**  
Once or twice a week is enough. Scrub damp feet gently, focusing on the heels, rinse, dry well and follow with foot cream or foot butter. Do not scrub broken or sore skin.

**Should I wear socks after foot cream?**  
At night, yes: a layer of foot cream or foot butter under clean cotton socks helps it stay on your feet rather than the sheets. During the day, let the cream sink in for a few minutes before putting on socks and shoes.

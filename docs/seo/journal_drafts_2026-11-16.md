## Body wash or bath bar? Natural body care for dry skin

/blogs/journal/natural-body-wash-guide · SEO title: Natural Body Wash or Bath Bar for Dry Skin | Swansura

Hot showers, central heating and cold wind all leave skin feeling tight in winter, and the wash you use is the first place to make a change. A **natural body wash** or a simple **bath bar** made from plant oils can clean skin without that squeaky, stripped feel, especially when it is followed by a good **body lotion for dry skin**. Here is how the two compare, what is inside the ones we stock, and a simple shower routine for dry skin.

### Body wash or bath bar: what is the difference?

A **bath bar** is true soap: plant oils and butters turned into soap with lye, which is why sodium hydroxide appears on the ingredient list. None of it is left in the finished bar. Bars are compact, last a long time on a draining dish and come with little or no plastic.
A **body wash** is a liquid made with mild cleansers, usually derived from coconut or sugar, plus water-based ingredients such as plant waters (hydrosols) and glycerin. It is easy to share, works well on a loofah and is simple to use on dry or sensitive skin.
Neither is better for every skin. If your skin feels tight after showering, try a creamy wash or a butter-rich bar, keep showers short, and always follow with a lotion.

### A shower routine for dry skin


- **Keep the water warm, not hot,** and the shower short.
- **Use a little wash or a light lather from the bar,** and focus on the areas that need it.
- **Exfoliate once or twice a week, not every day.** Our [body scrub guide](/blogs/journal/how-to-use-body-scrub) explains how.
- **Pat skin almost dry** with a towel instead of rubbing.
- **Apply a body lotion straight away,** while skin is still slightly damp.

### Good to know


- **Let bars dry between uses** on a draining dish so they stay firm and last longer.

- **Skin that stays very dry, itchy, cracked or sore** despite moisturising is worth showing to a pharmacist or your GP.
